## Day 2: Bullet-point formula (no product link)

> **Action verb + what you did + the measurable result**

## Day 3: Tailoring per job
**Reddit-style helpful answer** (for "I've applied to 100 jobs and heard nothing, what am I doing wrong?")
> A few things that usually explain silence:
> 1. **One resume for every job.** Take 15 minutes per application to mirror the posting's top 5–8 skills, using its exact wording, wherever it's true for you. Put them in your summary and in your most recent role.
> 2. **Formatting that ATS can't read.** Tables, text boxes, columns and headers or footers can scramble your content. A simple single-column layout is safer.
> 3. **Duties instead of results.** Swap "responsible for X" for "did X, which led to Y".
> 4. **Applying late.** Many postings get most applicants in the first few days. Turn on job alerts and apply early.
> 5. **No referrals.** For every application, try to message one person at the company. Even a short note helps.
>
> If you share a (redacted) bullet or two, I'm happy to suggest rewrites.

## Day 4: Cover letter openers
**LinkedIn**
> Stop opening cover letters with "I am writing to apply for…". They know.
>
> Openers that work better:
> • A specific reason: "Your team's move to self-serve onboarding is exactly the problem I solved at ___."
> • A result: "Last year I cut our support backlog by a third, and I'd like to do the same for ___."
> • A connection: "___ on your design team suggested I reach out."
>
> Then keep it under 300 words: why them, why you (2 proof points), and a simple close.

## Day 5: Career changers
**LinkedIn**
> Changing careers? Your resume should be organized around the job you want, not the job you had.
>
> 1. Open with a 2-line summary naming the target role and your 3 most transferable skills.
> 2. Retitle sections: "Relevant experience" before "Other experience".
> 3. Translate jargon: a teacher's "differentiated instruction" becomes a trainer's "adapting training to different skill levels".
> 4. Add projects, courses or volunteer work that use the new field's tools.
>
> Your experience counts. It just needs translating.
