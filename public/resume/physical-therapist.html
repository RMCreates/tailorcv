How to tailor a physical therapist resume to the job posting

Physical therapy employers look first for licensure and setting fit. Outpatient orthopedics, acute care, inpatient rehab, home health and pediatrics all draw on different skills, and your resume should make your experience in each clear.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

List your DPT, state license, and any specialist certifications (OCS, NCS, PCS, SCS) or continuing education such as manual therapy, dry needling, vestibular rehab or certifications relevant to the setting. ATS filters often scan for these along with EMR systems such as WebPT or Epic.

Hiring managers look for caseload and patient volume, the populations and conditions you treated, documentation and billing compliance, and outcomes you can honestly describe, such as functional goals met or patient retention. Clinical instructor experience and program development are strong signals for senior roles.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • DPT
  • State PT license
  • Orthopedic rehabilitation
  • Manual therapy
  • Therapeutic exercise
  • Patient evaluation and plan of care
  • Documentation (WebPT, Epic)
  • Medicare compliance / billing
  • Patient education
  • Clinical instructor (CI)

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Managed an outpatient orthopedic caseload of 12–14 patients per day, including post-surgical knee and shoulder rehabilitation.
  • Example: Served as clinical instructor for three DPT students during their final clinical rotations.
  • Example: Developed a vestibular rehabilitation program at the clinic after completing a certification course, giving patients a local referral option.

Common mistakes on physical therapist resumes

  • Not naming the setting and patient population for each role.
  • Leaving out certifications and continuing education.
  • Generic statements about "helping patients" without clinical specifics.
