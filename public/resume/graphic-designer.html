How to tailor a graphic designer resume to the job posting

For graphic designers, the portfolio does most of the persuading, but the resume decides whether anyone opens it. It needs to match the kind of design work in the posting and make your portfolio link impossible to miss.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Put your portfolio URL in the header and check that it works. ATS filters look for tools (Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign, Figma, After Effects) and work types (branding, print, packaging, social, motion, presentation design). Use the posting's terms; "brand identity" and "marketing collateral" are different jobs.

Hiring managers look for the range and volume of work you produced, the brands or clients you served, how you worked with copywriters, marketers and printers, and whether you can work within brand guidelines and deadlines. Keep the resume layout clean and readable; an overly designed resume can confuse ATS parsing.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Adobe Creative Suite (Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign)
  • Figma
  • Brand identity
  • Typography and layout
  • Print production / prepress
  • Social media graphics
  • Motion graphics / After Effects
  • Brand guidelines
  • Marketing collateral
  • Presentation design

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Designed packaging for a 14-product snack line, preparing print-ready files and working with the printer through proofs.
  • Example: Created and maintained the brand guidelines for a regional bank, used by its in-house team and two external agencies.
  • Example: Produced roughly 30 social graphics and two short animations per month for a retail brand's campaigns.

Common mistakes on graphic designer resumes

  • Missing or broken portfolio links.
  • Heavy visual layouts with text in images, columns or graphics that ATS software cannot read.
  • Listing design tools you only know at a beginner level.
