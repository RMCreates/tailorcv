How to tailor a bartender resume to the job posting

Bar managers hire on speed, accuracy, responsible service and sales. A bartender resume works best when it names the type of bar, the volume you handled, your certifications and the systems you ran, instead of describing your personality.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

State the setting for each job: neighborhood bar, high-volume nightclub, craft cocktail bar, hotel bar, restaurant bar, brewery taproom or catering and events. Give the volume (guests per night, number of wells, sales per shift if you know them) and whether you worked service bar, main bar or both. Hiring managers read this first because a club bartender and a cocktail-bar bartender are different hires.

List your certifications and permits: TIPS, ServSafe Alcohol, state or city server permits (RBS in California, TABC in Texas, MAST in Washington and so on), food handler card and any cocktail or spirits courses (BarSmarts, WSET, Cicerone for beer). Responsible service and ID checking should be stated, not implied.

Then show skills with specifics: classic and craft cocktail knowledge, speed-pouring and free-pour accuracy, wine and beer knowledge, batching and prep, inventory counts and ordering, bar cost control, cash handling, opening and closing procedures and POS systems (Toast, Aloha, Square, Micros). Mention upselling results, menu contributions, training new bartenders and any guest-service recognition.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • TIPS / ServSafe Alcohol / state server permit
  • Responsible alcohol service and ID checks
  • High-volume service
  • Craft cocktails and classics
  • Wine and beer knowledge
  • POS (Toast, Aloha, Square, Micros)
  • Cash handling and closing
  • Inventory and bar cost control
  • Bar prep and batching
  • Upselling and guest experience

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Worked the main bar at a 300-capacity music venue, serving 400+ guests on show nights with cash and card tabs balanced at close.
  • Example: Developed six seasonal cocktails for a 60-seat cocktail bar's menu, two of which became top-five sellers by volume.
  • Example: Ran weekly liquor inventory and ordering for a restaurant bar, keeping pour cost at about 19% against a 21% target.

Common mistakes on bartender resumes

  • Not naming the bar type and volume, so the manager cannot tell if you can handle their rush.
  • Leaving out server permits and responsible-service certifications that are legally required in many states.
  • Describing yourself as "outgoing" and "fun" instead of showing sales, speed and accuracy.
  • Omitting POS, inventory and closing responsibilities.
