How to tailor a insurance agent resume to the job posting

Insurance agencies and carriers hire licensed agents who can quote, bind and retain business in the lines they sell. The resume needs to state your licenses and lines of authority clearly and then show production, retention and the systems you work in.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Lead with licensing: Property & Casualty, Life & Health, or both, the states where you are licensed, and any designations (CISR, CIC, CPCU, CLU, ChFC). Then specify what you sell: personal lines (auto, home, renters, umbrella), commercial lines (BOP, general liability, workers' comp, commercial auto), life, health, Medicare or benefits. If you are a captive agent, name the carrier; if independent, name the carriers you are appointed with.

Give production numbers: new business premium written, policies per month, close rate on quotes, cross-sell or multi-line ratio, retention rate and book of business size. Agencies also care about service work: renewals, endorsements, certificates of insurance, claims assistance and remarketing accounts at renewal.

Name your software: agency management systems (Applied Epic, EZLynx, HawkSoft, AMS360, Vertafore), comparative raters, carrier portals and CRMs (Salesforce, HubSpot). Mention lead sources, telephone and in-person sales skills and compliance with state regulations and E&O practices.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Property & Casualty / Life & Health license
  • Personal lines / commercial lines
  • Quoting and binding
  • New business premium
  • Policy retention and renewals
  • Cross-selling and account rounding
  • Agency management system (Applied Epic, EZLynx, HawkSoft)
  • Carrier appointments
  • Claims assistance
  • Lead follow-up and CRM

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Wrote about $650K in new personal lines premium in a year as an independent agent appointed with six carriers, with a quote-to-bind rate near 40%.
  • Example: Serviced a book of 1,100 personal and small commercial accounts, handling renewals, endorsements and certificates with retention above the agency average.
  • Example: Passed the Life & Health exam within 90 days of hire and added life and umbrella policies to existing auto and home clients, raising the multi-line ratio.

Common mistakes on insurance agent resumes

  • Not stating your lines of authority and licensed states in the first few lines.
  • Leaving out premium, policy counts and retention, which agencies use to compare candidates.
  • Omitting the agency management system and raters you work in.
  • Describing service work vaguely when the posting is for an account manager or CSR hybrid role.
