How to tailor a data analyst resume to the job posting

A data analyst resume has to prove two things quickly: that you can get and clean data with the tools the team uses, and that your analysis led someone to make a decision. The second part is where most analyst resumes fall short.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Recruiters and ATS filters scan for the tool stack first: SQL is almost always required, followed by a spreadsheet tool, a BI tool such as Tableau, Power BI or Looker, and often Python or R. Name the exact tools from the posting, and mention the SQL dialect or warehouse (BigQuery, Snowflake, Redshift) if you know it.

Hiring managers then want the business context. Who used your dashboard or report, what question did it answer, and what changed? "Built 12 dashboards" says little; "built the weekly churn dashboard the retention team uses to prioritize outreach" says a lot. Domain experience (marketing, finance, operations, healthcare) is worth stating plainly because many analyst roles are domain-heavy.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • SQL
  • Excel / Google Sheets (pivot tables, lookups)
  • Tableau / Power BI / Looker
  • Python (pandas) or R
  • Data cleaning and validation
  • A/B testing
  • KPI reporting
  • Stakeholder communication
  • ETL / data pipelines
  • Statistical analysis

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Built a Power BI dashboard tracking weekly churn by plan and region; the retention team now uses it to choose which accounts to contact first.
  • Example: Rewrote 30 legacy Excel reports as scheduled SQL queries in Snowflake, saving the finance team roughly six hours of manual work per week.
  • Example: Analyzed a checkout A/B test and found the lift was limited to mobile users, which led product to ship the change to mobile only.

Common mistakes on data analyst resumes

  • Listing tools with no evidence of what you did with them.
  • Describing analysis without the decision or outcome it informed.
  • Using vague terms like "big data" or "advanced analytics" instead of the specific methods and tools the posting names.
