How to tailor a cybersecurity analyst resume to the job posting

Cybersecurity analyst roles cover security operations (SOC), vulnerability management, incident response, governance and compliance, and more. Your resume should match the specific area the posting describes and show hands-on work with real tooling.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters often scan for certifications (Security+, CySA+, CISSP, GIAC certifications such as GCIH or GSEC, CEH), tools (Splunk, Microsoft Sentinel, CrowdStrike, Tenable/Nessus, Qualys, Wireshark), and frameworks (NIST CSF, MITRE ATT&CK, ISO 27001, SOC 2). Security clearance status is often a hard requirement for government roles; list it if you have it.

Hiring managers want to know what you monitored, investigated or fixed: alert triage volume, incidents handled, detections written, vulnerabilities remediated, or audits supported. Be careful not to disclose sensitive details about past employers' security incidents or environments.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • SIEM (Splunk, Sentinel)
  • Incident response
  • Threat detection and alert triage
  • Vulnerability management (Nessus, Qualys)
  • EDR (CrowdStrike, Defender)
  • MITRE ATT&CK
  • NIST CSF / ISO 27001
  • Security+ / CySA+ / CISSP
  • Log analysis
  • Phishing analysis

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Triaged SIEM alerts in Splunk as part of a 24/7 SOC, escalating confirmed incidents and documenting investigations in the case management system.
  • Example: Wrote and tuned detection rules mapped to MITRE ATT&CK techniques, reducing false-positive alerts for the team.
  • Example: Ran monthly vulnerability scans with Tenable and tracked remediation with system owners, focusing on internet-facing critical findings.

Common mistakes on cybersecurity analyst resumes

  • Listing certifications without any hands-on experience to support them.
  • Revealing sensitive details about a previous employer's incidents or infrastructure.
  • Using generic "ensured security" statements instead of concrete actions and tools.
