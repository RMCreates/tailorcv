How to tailor a chef resume to the job posting

Restaurants and hospitality groups hire chefs on the kitchens they have run, the food they cook and whether they can hit food and labor cost targets. A chef resume should read like a track record of kitchens, not a list of culinary skills.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

For each kitchen, state the concept and cuisine, service style, covers per night or per week, seating and the size of the brigade you led. Name your position accurately (executive chef, chef de cuisine, sous chef, pastry chef) and who you reported to. Recruiters look for progression through those titles and for well-known kitchens, hotel groups or awards, but a small kitchen with strong numbers is just as credible.

Then give the business results: food cost and labor cost percentages you achieved against target, menu changes and their effect on sales or margin, waste reduction, successful health inspections and any volume milestones such as banquets or events served. Mention systems and certifications: ServSafe Manager, HACCP, allergen management, inventory and ordering platforms, recipe costing tools and POS.

Hiring managers also want leadership evidence: hiring and training cooks, scheduling, reducing turnover, developing a sous chef into a chef, and opening new locations. Keep a short, factual format; a chef resume with two pages of adjectives about passion reads poorly.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Menu development and recipe costing
  • Food cost and labor cost control
  • Kitchen leadership and training
  • ServSafe Manager / HACCP
  • Inventory, ordering and vendor management
  • High-volume service (covers per night)
  • Health inspections and food safety
  • Allergen management
  • Banquet and catering execution
  • Restaurant openings

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Ran a 14-person brigade as chef de cuisine at a 120-seat farm-to-table restaurant doing 250 covers on weekend nights, holding food cost at 29% against a 30% target.
  • Example: Rewrote the seasonal menu four times a year, raising average check by about 8% while cutting waste through whole-animal and preservation programs.
  • Example: Opened a second location as executive chef, hiring and training 22 kitchen staff and passing the first health inspection with no violations.

Common mistakes on chef resumes

  • Leaving out covers, brigade size and cost percentages, which are the numbers owners care about.
  • Listing cooking techniques instead of kitchens, roles and results.
  • Inflating titles (calling a line cook role "sous chef"), which references will contradict.
  • Omitting food safety certifications and inspection history.
