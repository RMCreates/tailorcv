How to tailor a teacher resume to the job posting

Teaching resumes are read by principals and district HR staff who need to know, fast, whether you are certified for the grade and subject and whether you can manage a classroom and move students forward.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

State your certification or license, subject area and grade band near the top, along with any endorsements (ESL/ELL, special education, reading specialist). District application systems often filter on these. Name the curricula and frameworks you have used, such as Common Core-aligned units, IB, AP or specific reading programs, if they match the posting.

Principals look for classroom management, differentiated instruction, use of data to adjust teaching, and collaboration with families and colleagues. Concrete details help: class sizes, student populations you have served, assessment results you can honestly describe, and extracurricular or committee work. Keep student information anonymous.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • State teaching certification
  • Differentiated instruction
  • Classroom management
  • Lesson planning / curriculum design
  • Formative and summative assessment
  • IEP / 504 accommodations
  • ESL / ELL support
  • Google Classroom / LMS
  • Parent communication
  • Data-driven instruction

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Taught 7th-grade math to five sections of 28 students, using weekly exit tickets to regroup students for targeted small-group practice.
  • Example: Co-planned with the special education teacher to implement IEP accommodations for 9 students in an inclusion classroom.
  • Example: Started an after-school robotics club that grew from 6 to 22 students and competed in the regional tournament in its second year.

Common mistakes on teacher resumes

  • Writing a generic philosophy statement instead of concrete classroom evidence.
  • Omitting certification details or grade levels.
  • Using identifiable student information or unverifiable test-score claims.
