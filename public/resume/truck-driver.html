How to tailor a truck driver resume to the job posting

Carriers and recruiters screen driver resumes against DOT and insurance requirements first: license class, endorsements, verifiable experience and a safe record. If those are clear in the first ten seconds, the rest of the resume gets read.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Put your CDL class (A or B), issuing state and endorsements (H hazmat, N tanker, X combination, T doubles/triples, P passenger) at the top, along with your DOT medical card status and TWIC card if you have one. Say how many years of verifiable commercial driving you have, because most insurers require a minimum, and state your driving record plainly: accident-free years, clean MVR, no preventables.

Then describe the driving itself. Recruiters want the route type (OTR, regional, dedicated, local, LTL, intermodal), the equipment (dry van, reefer, flatbed, tanker, step deck, doubles) and the freight (general, temperature-controlled, hazmat, oversize). Include approximate miles per year, average length of haul, whether you did touch or no-touch freight, and experience with ELDs, pre-trip and post-trip inspections and hours-of-service compliance.

Mention anything that reduces risk for the carrier: securement training for flatbed, mountain or winter driving, chaining, team driving, trainer or mentor roles, and any customer-facing delivery experience. Keep employment dates exact; DOT verification will check them.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • CDL Class A / Class B
  • Endorsements (H, N, X, T)
  • Clean MVR / accident-free record
  • DOT medical card
  • Hours-of-service (HOS) and ELD compliance
  • Pre-trip and post-trip inspections
  • OTR / regional / local routes
  • Dry van / reefer / flatbed
  • Load securement
  • On-time delivery

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Drove OTR dry van and reefer freight for a 48-state carrier, averaging about 120,000 miles per year with no preventable accidents over five years.
  • Example: Hauled flatbed steel and lumber on regional routes, securing loads with chains and straps to FMCSA standards with zero securement violations at roadside inspections.
  • Example: Ran a dedicated local grocery route with 8–12 stops per day, consistently meeting delivery windows and maintaining accurate ELD logs.

Common mistakes on truck driver resumes

  • Leaving off endorsements, years of verifiable experience or your safety record, which carriers must confirm before anything else.
  • Gaps or vague dates in employment history, which fail DOT background checks.
  • Not stating the route type and equipment, so recruiters cannot tell if you fit a flatbed, reefer or local posting.
  • Including a long objective statement instead of the credentials up front.
