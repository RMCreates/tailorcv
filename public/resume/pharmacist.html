How to tailor a pharmacist resume to the job posting

Pharmacist resumes are screened on licensure and practice setting first. Retail, hospital, clinical, specialty and long-term care pharmacy require different skills, and employers want to see that your background fits theirs.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Show your PharmD, state license(s), and relevant certifications such as immunization certification, BCPS, or other board certifications. ATS filters often look for terms like medication therapy management (MTM), verification, compounding, sterile or non-sterile compounding (USP <797>/<795>), immunizations and specific pharmacy systems.

Hiring managers look for volume handled, the practice setting, patient counseling, collaboration with prescribers, and any leadership such as supervising technicians or acting as pharmacist-in-charge. For hospital and clinical roles, residency training (PGY1/PGY2) and clinical services such as anticoagulation or antimicrobial stewardship are key.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • PharmD
  • State pharmacist license
  • Prescription verification
  • Medication therapy management
  • Patient counseling
  • Immunizations
  • Sterile compounding (USP <797>)
  • Drug interaction screening
  • Pharmacy technician supervision
  • Regulatory compliance

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Verified and dispensed prescriptions in a high-volume community pharmacy while supervising four technicians.
  • Example: Administered immunizations and ran seasonal flu clinics with local employers.
  • Example: Participated in the hospital's antimicrobial stewardship program, recommending de-escalation of therapy based on culture results.

Common mistakes on pharmacist resumes

  • Not making the practice setting obvious for each role.
  • Omitting residency or board certification details for clinical roles.
  • Listing duties every pharmacist performs without anything that distinguishes you.
