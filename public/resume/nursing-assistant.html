How to tailor a nursing assistant (CNA) resume to the job posting

Facilities hire CNAs who are on the state registry, reliable and experienced with the patient population they serve. A nursing assistant resume should settle the certification question in the first line and then show where you have worked, how many residents you cared for and what you did for them.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Put your CNA certification and state registry status at the top, with BLS/CPR and any additional credentials such as CMA (medication aide), home health aide, restorative aide, dementia or hospice training. If your certification is active in more than one state, list each one. Note your training program and clinical hours if you are newly certified.

For each job, name the setting (skilled nursing facility, assisted living, hospital unit, home health, hospice, memory care) and your typical assignment: number of residents or patients, shift and the acuity level. Describe the care you provide: activities of daily living, vital signs, repositioning and transfers with lifts, feeding assistance, intake and output, charting in systems like PointClickCare, MatrixCare or Epic, and reporting changes to the nurse.

Directors of nursing look for dependable attendance, infection control practice, resident safety (falls, skin integrity), compassion with dementia residents and teamwork. Mention charge or preceptor responsibilities, perfect attendance, and survey or inspection participation if you were part of it.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • CNA certification (state registry)
  • BLS / CPR
  • Activities of daily living (ADLs)
  • Vital signs and reporting
  • Safe transfers and mechanical lifts
  • Infection control
  • Charting (PointClickCare, MatrixCare, Epic)
  • Dementia and memory care
  • Long-term care / hospital / home health (setting)
  • Resident safety and falls prevention

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Provided care for 10–12 residents per shift on a 60-bed skilled nursing unit, including ADLs, vitals, transfers with Hoyer and sit-to-stand lifts and charting in PointClickCare.
  • Example: Worked nights in a 24-bed memory care neighborhood using redirection techniques, with no resident falls on my assignment in the past year.
  • Example: Floated to a hospital med-surg unit as a patient care technician, taking vitals, blood glucose checks and EKGs for a 28-bed unit.

Common mistakes on nursing assistant (CNA) resumes

  • Not stating your certification state and registry status, or leaving the expiration date off.
  • Listing "patient care" without the setting, resident count or specific tasks.
  • Leaving out attendance and reliability when facilities prize them.
  • Including unrelated jobs in detail while compressing the care experience.
