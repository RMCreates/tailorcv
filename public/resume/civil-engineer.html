How to tailor a civil engineer resume to the job posting

Civil engineering hiring is heavily shaped by licensure, project type and software. A transportation firm, a land development consultancy and a public works department will each read your resume for different experience.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Make your licensure status clear: PE (and states), EIT/EI, or FE passed. ATS filters often screen on these, plus tools such as AutoCAD Civil 3D, MicroStation, OpenRoads, HEC-RAS, StormCAD or SAP2000, depending on discipline. Name your specialty (transportation, water resources, structural, geotechnical, site development).

Hiring managers look for the types and sizes of projects you worked on, your role (design, review, construction administration, inspection), the codes and agency standards you followed (for example a state DOT manual or local stormwater regulations), and whether you worked directly with clients and permitting agencies.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • PE / EIT
  • AutoCAD Civil 3D
  • Site design and grading
  • Stormwater management
  • Roadway / transportation design
  • Construction administration
  • Permitting
  • Plan review
  • HEC-RAS or hydraulic modeling
  • Cost estimating

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Prepared grading, drainage and utility plans in Civil 3D for a 40-acre residential subdivision, from schematic design through permit approval.
  • Example: Designed stormwater detention for a commercial site to meet county release-rate requirements and prepared the drainage report.
  • Example: Provided construction administration on a $3M roadway widening project, reviewing submittals and responding to contractor RFIs.

Common mistakes on civil engineer resumes

  • Omitting licensure status or listing it vaguely.
  • Not describing project type and size.
  • Listing software without the deliverables you produced with it.
