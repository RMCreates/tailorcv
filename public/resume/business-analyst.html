How to tailor a business analyst resume to the job posting

Business analysts sit between the people who need something and the people who build it. Your resume should show that you can uncover real requirements, document them clearly, and see a change through to adoption.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters for BA roles often look for requirements gathering, user stories, process mapping (BPMN, swimlanes), gap analysis, UAT, and tools such as Jira, Confluence, Visio, SQL and sometimes Power BI. Certifications such as CBAP or the IIBA ECBA, or an Agile BA background, can help when the posting mentions them.

Hiring managers look for the systems and domains you worked in (ERP, CRM, banking, insurance, healthcare), the stakeholders you managed, and results: a process made faster, errors reduced, a system launched that people actually used. Show that you can work with data, not just documents.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Requirements gathering
  • User stories / acceptance criteria
  • Process mapping (BPMN)
  • Gap analysis
  • User acceptance testing (UAT)
  • Stakeholder management
  • Jira / Confluence
  • SQL
  • Data analysis
  • Agile / Scrum

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Gathered requirements from claims, finance and IT for a new claims-intake system, writing 80 user stories with acceptance criteria.
  • Example: Mapped the current and future state of the vendor onboarding process, identifying three duplicate approval steps that were then removed.
  • Example: Coordinated UAT with 12 business users, triaging defects in Jira and signing off the release with the product owner.

Common mistakes on business analyst resumes

  • Describing documentation work without showing what changed as a result.
  • Using generic "liaised with stakeholders" without naming who or why.
  • Omitting the domain, which is often a deciding factor for BA hiring.
