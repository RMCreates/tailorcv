How to tailor a warehouse associate resume to the job posting

Warehouse hiring moves quickly, and employers look for a short, clear resume that shows you are reliable, safe, and experienced with the equipment and tasks the job involves.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters and recruiters often check for tasks such as picking, packing, shipping and receiving, loading and unloading, inventory counts, and equipment: forklift, pallet jack, reach truck, order picker, and RF scanners. If you hold a forklift certification or OSHA training, list it near the top.

Hiring managers look for reliability (attendance, shift flexibility, willingness to work overtime or peak seasons), accuracy, productivity rates if you know them, safety record, and the type of warehouse you worked in, such as e-commerce fulfillment, cold storage, or distribution. Physical requirements like lifting are often in the posting; you do not need to repeat them unless asked.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Order picking and packing
  • Shipping and receiving
  • Forklift / pallet jack operation
  • RF scanner
  • Inventory counts
  • Loading and unloading trucks
  • Warehouse management system (WMS)
  • Safety procedures / OSHA
  • Quality checks
  • Shift flexibility

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Picked and packed online orders using an RF scanner in a busy e-commerce fulfillment center, regularly meeting or exceeding the hourly rate target.
  • Example: Operated a certified forklift to unload inbound trailers and put away pallets in the correct locations in the WMS.
  • Example: Worked mandatory overtime throughout the holiday peak season with no missed shifts.

Common mistakes on warehouse associate resumes

  • Leaving out equipment you can operate or certifications you hold.
  • Long paragraphs instead of short, scannable bullets.
  • Not mentioning shift availability when the posting emphasizes it.
