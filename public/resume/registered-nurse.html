How to tailor a registered nurse resume to the job posting

Nursing resumes are screened for credentials first and clinical fit second. A hiring manager filling a telemetry position wants to see telemetry, the right certifications and the right patient population before reading anything else.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Put your license prominently: RN, the state(s) or compact license status, and license number if the employer asks for it. List current certifications such as BLS, ACLS, PALS, NRP or specialty certifications (CCRN, CEN, OCN) with expiry dates. ATS filters frequently screen on these exact acronyms.

For each role, state the unit type, bed count or patient ratio, and patient population, then the EHR you used (Epic, Cerner, Meditech). Hiring managers look for clinical skills that match the unit, plus evidence of patient safety, education, precepting, and involvement in quality projects. Keep bullets factual and specific; avoid inflated language about "saving lives".

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • RN license (state / compact)
  • BLS / ACLS / PALS
  • Patient assessment
  • Medication administration
  • Epic / Cerner EHR
  • Care planning
  • Patient and family education
  • Telemetry / critical care / med-surg (as relevant)
  • Infection control
  • Interdisciplinary collaboration

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Provided care for 5–6 patients per shift on a 32-bed med-surg unit, including post-operative and telemetry-monitored patients.
  • Example: Precepted 8 new graduate nurses through a 12-week orientation and helped revise the unit's orientation checklist.
  • Example: Joined the unit's falls-prevention committee and helped roll out hourly rounding, contributing to fewer reported falls over the following year.

Common mistakes on registered nurse resumes

  • Burying your license and certifications at the bottom of page two.
  • Not naming the unit type and patient population for each job.
  • Including expired certifications without marking them, or omitting expiry dates the employer needs to verify.
