How to tailor a police officer resume to the job posting

Police hiring runs through a formal process with background checks and fixed requirements, so your resume has to be exact and verifiable. Lead with certification and academy status, then describe assignments, training and results in plain, factual language.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

State your status clearly: certified (POST or state equivalent) with the issuing state and year, academy completed or sponsorship needed, and whether you are a lateral transfer. List firearms qualification, emergency vehicle operations, CPR/first aid, crisis intervention team (CIT) training, defensive tactics, CJIS/NCIC access, and any instructor or specialist certifications (FTO, DRE, SFST, radar, less-lethal). Include education, military service and language skills.

For each department or agency, describe assignments and the environment: patrol in a specified population or beat type, traffic, investigations, community policing, school resource, K-9, SWAT, detective or specialized units. Describe the work factually: calls for service handled, arrests and case clearance where appropriate, reports and court testimony, use of body-worn cameras, and community programs you ran.

Background investigators and hiring panels look for integrity, consistent dates, commendations and training records. Keep claims measured and exact; anything on the resume may be verified in the background phase. If you are a first-time applicant, emphasize relevant experience such as military, corrections, security, dispatch or community service, plus physical fitness and a clean record.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • POST / state peace officer certification
  • Academy completion (or lateral status)
  • Patrol and calls for service
  • Report writing and court testimony
  • Investigations and case clearance
  • Firearms qualification
  • Crisis intervention (CIT) and de-escalation
  • Community policing
  • CJIS / NCIC
  • Field training officer (FTO) or instructor roles

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Patrolled a mid-sized suburban beat, handling about 20 calls for service per shift, with reports regularly cited as accurate and complete by supervising sergeants and prosecutors.
  • Example: Served as a field training officer for six probationary officers over three years and contributed to updating the department's FTO manual.
  • Example: Completed CIT training and handled mental-health-related calls as the designated responder, coordinating with the county crisis team to divert cases from arrest where appropriate.

Common mistakes on police officer resumes

  • Unclear certification or academy status, which determines eligibility for the posting.
  • Vague descriptions of assignments instead of beat, unit and the nature of the work.
  • Any inconsistency in dates, titles or training that the background check will expose.
  • Overstating involvement in arrests or investigations.
