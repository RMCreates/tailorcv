How to tailor a firefighter resume to the job posting

Fire departments hire against certification requirements and physical standards, then look for medical credentials and evidence that you work well in a crew. Your resume should make eligibility obvious in the first lines and then show training, calls and responsibility.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

List your certifications with the issuing body: Firefighter I and II (NFPA 1001, state or Pro Board/IFSAC), EMT-Basic, Advanced EMT or Paramedic with state and NREMT registration, HazMat Awareness and Operations, ICS/NIMS courses (100, 200, 700, 800), CPAT completion date, driver/operator or pumper certifications, wildland qualifications (S-130/S-190, red card) and any rescue specialties (vehicle extrication, rope, water). Add your valid driver's license and CPR.

For each department, state whether it was career, volunteer, paid-on-call, military or wildland, the department size and the call volume. Describe the work factually: structure fires, EMS runs, vehicle accidents, technical rescue, inspections, public education and apparatus maintenance. Mention any rank (engineer, lieutenant), acting roles and station duties you were trusted with.

Hiring boards also value related experience: military service, construction or trades, healthcare and EMS agencies, coaching, and community involvement. Keep physical fitness and a clean driving record visible, and make sure every date and certification is accurate; departments verify everything.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Firefighter I / II (NFPA 1001)
  • EMT-B / Paramedic (NREMT and state)
  • CPAT
  • HazMat Awareness / Operations
  • ICS / NIMS (100, 200, 700, 800)
  • Fire suppression and EMS response
  • Vehicle extrication and technical rescue
  • Driver/operator / apparatus
  • Wildland (S-130 / S-190)
  • Fire prevention and public education

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Responded to roughly 1,100 calls per year as a career firefighter/EMT at a three-station department, including structure fires, motor vehicle accidents and medical emergencies.
  • Example: Completed Firefighter I and II, HazMat Operations and EMT-B in a 16-week academy and passed the CPAT on the first attempt.
  • Example: Served four years as a volunteer firefighter, logging 300+ training hours and acting as driver/operator for the department's engine on about a third of calls.

Common mistakes on firefighter resumes

  • Leaving certifications undated or without the issuing body, so eligibility cannot be confirmed.
  • Not stating whether experience was career, volunteer or military.
  • Padding the resume with generic traits instead of training, calls and responsibilities.
  • Omitting EMS credentials, which most departments now require or strongly prefer.
