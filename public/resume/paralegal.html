How to tailor a paralegal resume to the job posting

Law firms and legal departments hire paralegals for a practice area, so the fastest way to get screened out is a resume that does not say which kind of law you support. Lead with practice area, the tasks you handle independently and the tools you use.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Name your practice areas (civil litigation, personal injury, corporate, real estate, family, immigration, intellectual property, employment, bankruptcy) and your education: a paralegal certificate (note if ABA-approved), degree, and any credential such as NALA's Certified Paralegal (CP), NFPA's CRP or a state certification. Notary commission is worth a line.

Describe your work at the level of the documents and processes you own. Litigation paralegals should mention drafting pleadings and discovery, managing document productions, deposition and trial preparation, exhibits, calendaring deadlines under court rules and e-filing. Corporate or transactional paralegals should mention entity formation, closing binders, due diligence, contract management and corporate records. Give the size of the matters or the number of attorneys you support.

List tools by name: Westlaw, Lexis, Relativity or Everlaw for e-discovery, Clio, NetDocuments, iManage, CompuLaw or other docketing software, and the court e-filing systems you use. Firms also look for billable-hours experience, confidentiality and a track record of meeting deadlines without reminders.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Civil litigation / corporate / real estate (practice area)
  • Certified Paralegal (NALA CP)
  • Drafting pleadings and discovery
  • Legal research (Westlaw, Lexis)
  • E-discovery (Relativity, Everlaw)
  • Docketing and deadline management
  • Trial and deposition preparation
  • E-filing (state and federal)
  • Document management (iManage, NetDocuments)
  • Client communication

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Supported three litigation attorneys on commercial disputes, drafting discovery requests and responses, managing document productions in Relativity and preparing exhibit binders for six trials.
  • Example: Maintained the docket for 90 active matters using CompuLaw with no missed court deadlines over two years.
  • Example: Handled entity formations, annual filings and closing checklists for a corporate group, managing records for about 150 subsidiaries.

Common mistakes on paralegal resumes

  • Leaving the practice area unclear, so the firm cannot tell if you fit the group that is hiring.
  • Describing tasks as "assisted attorneys" rather than naming the documents and processes you owned.
  • Omitting software, e-filing systems and research platforms.
  • Listing confidential client names or case details on the resume.
