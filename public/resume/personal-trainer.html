How to tailor a personal trainer resume to the job posting

Gyms and studios hire trainers who are certified, keep clients coming back and can sell sessions. Your resume should prove the certification quickly and then show client load, retention and results rather than a list of exercises you know.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Lead with your certification and who issued it: NASM-CPT, ACE, ACSM, NSCA-CPT or CSCS, ISSA, plus CPR/AED (required by nearly every employer) and any specializations such as corrective exercise, nutrition coaching, senior fitness, pre- and postnatal or youth training. Add a degree in exercise science or kinesiology if you have one. Many gyms screen out applications without a recognized certification.

Then describe your practice: number of active clients, sessions per week, client types (general population, athletes, older adults, rehab-adjacent with physician clearance), and the formats you deliver (one-on-one, small group, group fitness classes, online coaching). Mention assessment and programming methods, progress tracking and the software you use (Trainerize, TrueCoach, Mindbody, ABC Fitness).

Commercial gyms also hire on sales and retention: package or membership sales, consultation-to-client conversion, client retention over 6 or 12 months, and referrals. Studios and boutique gyms care about class attendance and community. State results you can support without medical claims: strength milestones, consistency, program adherence.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • NASM-CPT / ACE / ACSM / NSCA certification
  • CPR / AED
  • Fitness assessments and program design
  • Client retention
  • Session and package sales
  • Small group and group fitness instruction
  • Corrective exercise / nutrition coaching (specialization)
  • Progress tracking software (Trainerize, Mindbody)
  • Strength and conditioning
  • Client consultations

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Maintained a roster of 28 active clients delivering about 35 one-on-one sessions per week, with roughly 80% of clients continuing past 12 months.
  • Example: Converted about half of complimentary consultations into training packages, consistently ranking among the top three trainers at the club for monthly sales.
  • Example: Designed and led a 6-week strength program for members over 60 that filled all three sessions and generated 11 new personal-training clients.

Common mistakes on personal trainer resumes

  • Not naming the certifying body, or listing a certification without a current CPR/AED credential.
  • Leaving out client numbers, session volume and retention.
  • Ignoring the sales side when applying to commercial gyms that expect it.
  • Making medical or weight-loss claims about clients that you cannot substantiate.
