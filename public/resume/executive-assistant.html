How to tailor an executive assistant resume to the job posting

An executive assistant's value comes from extending the reach of the executive they support. Your resume should show the seniority of your executives, the complexity of what you managed, and the trust placed in you.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters look for complex calendar management, travel coordination, expense reporting (Concur, Expensify), board meeting preparation, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, and confidentiality. Many postings also mention project management or chief-of-staff-style responsibilities.

Hiring managers want to know who you supported (CEO, CFO, founders, a leadership team), how many executives at once, and anything beyond scheduling: preparing board materials, managing offsites, handling communications, running small projects or acting as a gatekeeper. Examples of discretion and judgment are more persuasive than adjectives.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • C-suite support
  • Complex calendar management
  • Domestic and international travel
  • Board meeting preparation
  • Expense reporting (Concur, Expensify)
  • Event and offsite planning
  • Confidentiality and discretion
  • Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace
  • Project coordination
  • Gatekeeping and prioritization

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Supported the CEO and CFO of a 400-person company, managing calendars across four time zones and coordinating international travel.
  • Example: Prepared and distributed quarterly board materials and coordinated logistics for in-person board meetings.
  • Example: Planned a two-day leadership offsite for 25 executives, handling venue, agenda logistics and budget.

Common mistakes on executive assistant resumes

  • Not stating whose office you supported.
  • Describing the role as "answered phones and scheduled meetings" when you did much more.
  • Including confidential details about your executive or company.
