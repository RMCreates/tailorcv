How to tailor a product manager resume to the job posting

Product manager resumes are hard to write because PMs rarely do the work alone. The challenge is showing what you personally decided and drove, while being honest that engineers and designers built it.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Recruiters screen for the type of product (B2B SaaS, consumer, platform, hardware), the stage (zero-to-one or scaling), and the domain. Mirror the posting's language: if it says "growth" or "platform" or "payments", show that experience prominently. ATS filters often catch terms such as roadmap, discovery, prioritization and go-to-market.

Hiring managers look for outcomes tied to metrics: adoption, retention, revenue, activation, or cost. They also want evidence of judgment: a problem you discovered through research, a trade-off you made, something you chose not to build. Use verbs like defined, prioritized, launched and led, and mention team size so readers understand your scope.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Product roadmap
  • Customer discovery / user research
  • Prioritization frameworks
  • Product requirements (PRDs)
  • Cross-functional leadership
  • Go-to-market
  • A/B testing
  • Product analytics (Amplitude, Mixpanel)
  • Agile / Scrum
  • OKRs / KPIs

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Defined and launched self-serve onboarding for a B2B analytics product with a team of 6 engineers and 1 designer, raising trial-to-paid conversion over two quarters.
  • Example: Ran 20 customer interviews that showed admins, not end users, were blocking adoption; reprioritized the roadmap around admin controls.
  • Example: Cut a planned feature after a two-week prototype test showed low usage, freeing a squad for the integration work sales had been requesting.

Common mistakes on product manager resumes

  • Writing "I built" for features engineers built. "Led" or "launched with" is more accurate and still strong.
  • Listing features shipped without saying why they mattered.
  • Ignoring the domain. A payments PM role will favour candidates who show payments, compliance or risk experience.
