How to tailor a radiologic technologist resume to the job posting

Imaging departments hire on ARRT credentials, state licensure and modality experience. The resume should make it immediately clear which modalities you are certified in and which exams you perform routinely, along with the equipment and systems you know.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Lead with your credentials: ARRT registration with the postprimary modalities you hold (R, CT, MR, M, BD, VI), your state license or permit, BLS/CPR and any venipuncture or contrast administration training. Note your degree and program accreditation. Many postings specify a modality, and ATS filters are set on the ARRT abbreviations.

For each facility, describe the setting (trauma center, community hospital, outpatient imaging center, orthopedic clinic, mobile unit), your shift, the exams you perform and the daily volume: general diagnostic, portable and OR work with C-arm, fluoroscopy, CT, MRI, mammography or interventional procedures. Name the equipment manufacturers (GE, Siemens, Philips, Canon, Hologic) and the PACS, RIS and EHR you use (Epic Radiant, Cerner, Sectra, Merge).

Managers also look for radiation safety practice (ALARA, shielding, dose tracking), patient positioning skill with difficult patients, image quality and repeat rates, protocol knowledge, quality assurance checks and cross-training willingness. Teaching students and on-call coverage are also worth stating.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • ARRT (R) with CT / MR / M postprimary (as held)
  • State radiologic technologist license
  • Patient positioning and image quality
  • Portable and OR imaging (C-arm)
  • Fluoroscopy
  • CT / MRI (if applicable)
  • Radiation safety / ALARA
  • PACS / RIS (Epic Radiant, Sectra)
  • Contrast administration and IV access
  • BLS / CPR

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Performed 40–50 diagnostic, portable and C-arm exams per shift at a Level II trauma center, covering the ED, OR and inpatient units on a rotating schedule.
  • Example: Cross-trained in CT and earned ARRT (CT) within a year, now covering CT call two nights a week on a Siemens 128-slice scanner.
  • Example: Kept my repeat rate under the department's target through consistent positioning and technique adjustments on GE digital radiography units.

Common mistakes on radiologic technologist resumes

  • Not listing ARRT modalities and state license in the first lines.
  • Omitting exam types, volume and the setting, which determine whether you fit the posting.
  • Leaving equipment, PACS and EHR systems unnamed.
  • Overlooking radiation safety and quality practices that managers screen for.
