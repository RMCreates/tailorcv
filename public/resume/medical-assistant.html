How to tailor a medical assistant resume to the job posting

Clinics hire medical assistants who can be productive quickly, so the resume needs to show certification, the clinical and administrative tasks you actually perform, the specialty and the EHR you know. A clear, well-organized one-page resume usually wins.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

State your credential and who issued it: CMA (AAMA), RMA (AMT), CCMA (NHA) or NCMA (NCCT), plus BLS/CPR and any phlebotomy or EKG certification. Many postings require a specific credential, and ATS filters are set on those acronyms. Include your program and externship if you are early in your career.

Describe the clinical work precisely: rooming patients, vitals, medication and vaccine administration (specify injections if allowed in your state), phlebotomy, EKGs, point-of-care testing, specimen collection, assisting with procedures, sterilizing instruments. Name the specialty (family medicine, pediatrics, cardiology, dermatology, orthopedics, urgent care) and the daily volume, since a 30-patient-a-day clinic is a different environment from a specialty office.

Then cover the administrative side: scheduling, check-in, insurance verification, prior authorizations, referrals, patient portal messages and the EHR you used (Epic, eClinicalWorks, athenahealth, NextGen, Cerner). Hiring managers also look for HIPAA awareness, bilingual ability and reliability.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • CMA (AAMA) / RMA / CCMA
  • BLS / CPR
  • Vital signs and patient rooming
  • Phlebotomy / venipuncture
  • Injections and immunizations
  • EKG
  • EHR (Epic, eClinicalWorks, athenahealth)
  • Insurance verification and prior authorizations
  • Scheduling and patient intake
  • HIPAA compliance

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Roomed 25–35 patients per day in a four-provider family medicine clinic, recording vitals, medication reconciliation and visit reason in Epic.
  • Example: Performed venipuncture, injections and EKGs for a cardiology practice and maintained the point-of-care testing log for CLIA-waived tests.
  • Example: Handled prior authorizations and referrals for two providers, cutting the backlog of pending authorizations from several weeks to a few days.

Common mistakes on medical assistant resumes

  • Not naming your certification body, or listing "medical assistant" without clarifying whether you are certified.
  • Omitting the EHR and the specialty, both of which clinics filter on.
  • Listing generic duties ("assisted doctors") instead of the specific clinical tasks you performed.
  • Forgetting externship experience when you have little paid experience; it counts.
