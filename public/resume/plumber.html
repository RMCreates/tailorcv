How to tailor a plumber resume to the job posting

Plumbing employers hire on license, code knowledge and the kind of plumbing you have done. A service plumber who diagnoses problems in occupied homes and a new-construction plumber roughing in a hospital are different hires, and your resume should make it obvious which one you are.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Lead with your license: apprentice with hours, journeyman or master, and the state or municipality that issued it. Add related credentials such as backflow prevention tester, medical gas installer (ASSE 6010), OSHA 10/30, gas fitting endorsements, confined-space training and any manufacturer training for water heaters, boilers or tankless systems. Recruiters and ATS filters search for these terms directly.

Next, make the type of work clear. For each job, say whether it was residential service and repair, remodel, new construction, commercial or industrial, and which systems you worked on: DWV, water supply, gas lines, water heaters, boilers, hydronic heating, sump and sewage pumps, fixtures, backflow devices. Name the materials you are fluent with (copper, PEX, PVC, CPVC, cast iron, black iron) and the code you work under (IPC or UPC, plus local amendments).

Service companies in particular want to see customer-facing skills and sales or ticket averages if you know them, plus a clean driving record. Construction employers want prints, layout, inspections passed and crew size.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Journeyman / Master Plumber license
  • IPC / UPC plumbing code
  • Service and repair diagnostics
  • DWV and water supply rough-in
  • Water heater and boiler installation
  • Gas line installation
  • Backflow prevention certification
  • PEX / copper / PVC
  • Drain cleaning and camera inspection
  • OSHA 10 / OSHA 30

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Ran 6–8 residential service calls per day for a home-services company, diagnosing and repairing leaks, water heaters and drain issues with a high rate of same-day fixes.
  • Example: Roughed in DWV and water supply for a 120-unit apartment building to IPC with no inspection corrections across four floors.
  • Example: Completed backflow tester certification and took over the company's annual testing contracts for 60 commercial accounts.

Common mistakes on plumber resumes

  • Not stating your license status and state in the first few lines.
  • Describing jobs as "plumbing duties" instead of the systems, materials and code you worked with.
  • Leaving out customer service and driving record for service roles where they matter most.
  • Listing expired certifications without a date, which employers will discover anyway.
