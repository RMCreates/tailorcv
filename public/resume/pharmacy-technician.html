How to tailor a pharmacy technician resume to the job posting

Pharmacies hire technicians on certification and registration first, then on whether you have worked the same kind of pharmacy and the same systems. Retail, hospital, mail-order and compounding are different jobs, and your resume should say which ones you have done.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

State your credentials in the first lines: PTCB CPhT or NHA ExCPT, state technician registration or license, immunization certification if your state allows technicians to administer vaccines, and sterile or non-sterile compounding training (USP 797/800). Include the expiry date and the year you earned the certification.

Then describe the pharmacy and your duties. Retail technicians should mention prescription volume per day, data entry, filling and verification support, insurance adjudication and rejections, prior authorizations, inventory and returns, controlled substance handling and the system used (RxConnect, Intercom Plus, EnterpriseRx, PioneerRx, Liberty). Hospital technicians should describe unit-dose filling, IV admixture and chemotherapy compounding, automated dispensing cabinets (Pyxis, Omnicell), carousels and the hospital pharmacy system (Epic Willow, Cerner, Meditech).

Pharmacists in charge look for accuracy, speed, customer service at the counter, a clean record on controlled substances and familiarity with HIPAA and board of pharmacy regulations. Any role in inventory management, 340B, medication reconciliation, tech-check-tech or training new technicians is worth a bullet.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • PTCB CPhT / ExCPT
  • State pharmacy technician registration
  • Prescription data entry and filling
  • Insurance adjudication and rejections
  • Prior authorizations
  • Sterile compounding (USP 797 / 800)
  • Automated dispensing (Pyxis, Omnicell)
  • Pharmacy software (RxConnect, Epic Willow, PioneerRx)
  • Inventory and controlled substance handling
  • Patient and customer service

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Processed about 350 prescriptions per day at a high-volume chain pharmacy, entering orders in RxConnect, resolving insurance rejections and managing prior authorizations.
  • Example: Compounded IV admixtures and TPN in a USP 797-compliant cleanroom at a 400-bed hospital and maintained Pyxis stock across 14 nursing units.
  • Example: Reconciled the controlled-substance inventory and perpetual log daily with no discrepancies over two years.

Common mistakes on pharmacy technician resumes

  • Leaving out your certification body and state registration, which pharmacies must verify before hiring.
  • Not specifying retail versus hospital versus mail-order experience.
  • Omitting the pharmacy software and automation you have used.
  • Describing duties vaguely ("assisted pharmacist") instead of naming the tasks and volume.
