How to tailor an operations manager resume to the job posting

Operations manager is one of the broadest titles in hiring. It can mean running a warehouse, a clinic, a restaurant group or a software company's internal processes. Your resume needs to make clear what kind of operation you ran and how you made it work better.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Recruiters and ATS filters look for the operating environment (manufacturing, logistics, retail, healthcare, SaaS) and the levers you managed: staffing, scheduling, budgets, vendors, inventory, quality, safety and process improvement. Methodologies such as Lean or Six Sigma, and tools such as an ERP or WMS, are frequent keywords.

Hiring managers look for scope (headcount, sites, budget, volume) and measurable change: cost per unit, throughput, on-time delivery, error rates, turnover or customer satisfaction. Show that you can both run the day-to-day and lead improvement projects.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Process improvement
  • Lean / Six Sigma
  • P&L or budget management
  • Team leadership and staffing
  • KPI tracking
  • Vendor management
  • Inventory control
  • Health and safety compliance
  • ERP systems
  • Cross-functional coordination

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Ran daily operations for a 90-person distribution center, managing four shift supervisors and a $5M operating budget.
  • Example: Led a Lean project to redesign the packing area layout, reducing walking distance and increasing orders packed per hour.
  • Example: Renegotiated three supplier contracts and consolidated deliveries, lowering inbound freight costs for the year.

Common mistakes on operations manager resumes

  • Not stating what kind of operation you ran.
  • Listing duties without scope numbers like headcount or budget.
  • Using "streamlined operations" without saying what changed and by how much.
