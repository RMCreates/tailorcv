How to tailor a electrician resume to the job posting

Electrical contractors and facilities teams screen for one thing before anything else: can you legally do the work in their state? License status, apprenticeship hours and the type of work you have actually wired come before everything else on the page.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

State your license level and state at the very top: apprentice (with hours completed), journeyman or master, plus the state or jurisdiction and any reciprocity. If you are still in a program, name it (IBEW/NECA JATC, ABC, IEC or a trade school) and the year you expect to test. Add OSHA 10 or OSHA 30, NFPA 70E arc-flash training, first aid/CPR and any manufacturer certifications. ATS keyword filters in this trade are usually set to license terms and safety credentials rather than soft skills.

Then be specific about the work. Recruiters want to know whether you have done residential service, new construction, commercial tenant improvement, industrial maintenance or low-voltage, because those are different jobs. For each employer, name the setting, the voltage range you worked with, the systems (panels, feeders, conduit, lighting controls, motor controls, generators, PLCs, fire alarm, solar) and the size of the projects or sites.

Hiring managers also look for evidence that you read prints, pull permits and pass inspections, troubleshoot rather than just install, and work safely without supervision. A clean record of passed inspections or a role running a small crew says more than a paragraph of adjectives.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Journeyman / Master Electrician license
  • NEC (National Electrical Code)
  • OSHA 10 / OSHA 30
  • NFPA 70E / arc flash
  • Conduit bending and installation
  • Panel and service installation
  • Blueprint and schematic reading
  • Motor controls / PLC
  • Troubleshooting and repair
  • Lockout/tagout (LOTO)

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Completed a 4-year IBEW apprenticeship (8,000 hours) and passed the state journeyman exam on the first attempt; now licensed in two states.
  • Example: Installed rough-in and finish electrical for roughly 40 single-family homes a year, including 200A services, with no failed inspections over three years.
  • Example: Troubleshot and repaired 480V motor control circuits and VFDs on a packaging line, reducing unplanned downtime calls to the maintenance team.

Common mistakes on electrician resumes

  • Leaving the license level, state or apprenticeship hours off the resume, or hiding them under education.
  • Writing "electrical work" without saying residential, commercial or industrial, or what voltage and systems you handled.
  • Listing every tool you own instead of the systems you installed and the problems you solved.
  • Omitting safety training and a safety record when the posting mentions OSHA or NFPA 70E.
