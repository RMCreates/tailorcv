How to tailor a security guard resume to the job posting

Security employers need to confirm you are licensed, trained and reliable before anything else. A good security guard resume states your license and training clearly, names the sites and post types you have worked and shows that you write accurate reports and stay calm under pressure.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Put your state guard license or registration (for example a California BSIS Guard Card, Texas Level II/III, New York Security Guard registration) at the top, including whether you are armed or unarmed and your firearm permit if applicable. Add CPR/first aid/AED, OSHA training, de-escalation or use-of-force training, and any specialized credentials such as fire safety director, CPP or PSP. Military or law enforcement background should be visible immediately.

For each assignment, describe the site type (corporate campus, hospital, retail loss prevention, residential, warehouse, data center, events, school) and the post duties: access control, visitor management, patrols on foot or vehicle, CCTV monitoring, alarm response, incident reporting, escorting, emergency response and enforcing post orders. Mention the systems you used: access control platforms, CCTV software, incident reporting apps, radio protocols and guard tour systems.

Supervisors look for a clean record, punctuality and schedule flexibility (nights, weekends), a valid driver's license, professional appearance and clear written communication. Any incident where your report or response was used by police, management or insurers is worth a factual bullet.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • State security guard license / guard card
  • Armed / unarmed (with permit)
  • Access control and visitor management
  • Patrols and site inspections
  • CCTV and alarm monitoring
  • Incident report writing
  • Emergency response
  • De-escalation and conflict management
  • CPR / first aid / AED
  • Post orders and compliance

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Controlled access for a 1,200-employee corporate headquarters, managing visitor badges, contractor check-in and after-hours alarm response on a 12-hour night shift.
  • Example: Conducted hourly foot and vehicle patrols of a 40-acre distribution site and documented all incidents in the client's reporting system, with reports used in three theft investigations.
  • Example: De-escalated recurring disputes at a downtown residential tower lobby and coordinated with police when required, with no use-of-force incidents in four years.

Common mistakes on security guard resumes

  • Leaving the license number, state and armed status off the resume.
  • Describing posts as "security duties" instead of naming the site type and specific responsibilities.
  • Gaps or inaccurate dates, which fail background checks.
  • No mention of report writing, which many supervisors treat as the core skill.
