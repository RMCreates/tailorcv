How to tailor a receptionist resume to the job posting

Front-desk hiring is fast, and the resume usually gets a short look. Employers want to see that you have handled a busy front desk, a multi-line phone system and scheduling, and that you present well and stay calm when several things happen at once.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Name the environment: corporate office, medical or dental practice, law firm, salon, hotel, school or multi-tenant building, plus the daily volume of calls and visitors. Describe the core duties specifically: answering and routing calls on a multi-line system, greeting and signing in visitors, issuing badges, managing conference rooms, handling mail and couriers, scheduling appointments, data entry and keeping the lobby and supplies in order.

List your tools: Microsoft Office or Google Workspace, phone systems (RingCentral, Cisco, Teams), scheduling and visitor software, and for medical offices the EHR or practice software (Epic, Dentrix, athenahealth) and insurance verification. Typing speed, bilingual ability and notary commission are all useful lines.

Employers also screen for soft but observable skills: punctuality, professional appearance, discretion with confidential information, and the ability to de-escalate frustrated callers or visitors. Mention any additional responsibilities you took on, such as supporting executives, processing invoices or training new front-desk staff.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Multi-line phone system
  • Greeting and visitor management
  • Appointment scheduling
  • Microsoft Office / Google Workspace
  • Calendar and conference room management
  • Mail, courier and deliveries
  • Data entry and records
  • Customer service and de-escalation
  • EHR / practice software (for medical offices)
  • Bilingual (if applicable)

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Answered and routed 100+ calls per day on a RingCentral system for a 12-attorney law firm while greeting clients and managing six conference rooms.
  • Example: Checked in 40–60 patients per day at a dermatology practice, verifying insurance and collecting copays in athenahealth with minimal check-in errors.
  • Example: Took over invoice intake and vendor badge management for the office manager, documenting both processes for coverage staff.

Common mistakes on receptionist resumes

  • Not stating call and visitor volume or the type of office, which is how employers judge fit.
  • Leaving out the phone system, scheduling tools and software you know.
  • Generic phrases like "excellent communication skills" with nothing to back them up.
  • A two-page resume for a role where one tight page is expected.
