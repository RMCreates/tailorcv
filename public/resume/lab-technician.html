How to tailor a lab technician resume to the job posting

Laboratory employers hire against credential requirements and the specific techniques, instruments and quality systems you have worked with. Whether you work in a clinical lab, a research lab or quality control for a manufacturer, the resume should name the bench work precisely.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Make your lab type and credentials clear. Clinical laboratory technicians should list ASCP certification (MLT or MLS), state license where required (California, New York, Florida and others), and the departments you have worked: hematology, chemistry, coagulation, urinalysis, microbiology, blood bank, molecular. Research or industrial technicians should list their degree and field, GLP or GMP experience, and techniques such as PCR, qPCR, ELISA, western blot, cell culture, chromatography (HPLC, GC), spectroscopy and titrations.

Name instruments and systems by model and vendor: Sysmex, Beckman Coulter, Roche cobas, Abbott Alinity, bioMérieux, Cepheid, as well as the LIS or LIMS (Epic Beaker, Cerner, Sunquest, LabWare). Describe sample volume, specimen processing and accessioning, quality control runs, calibration, maintenance logs and proficiency testing participation.

Supervisors look for accuracy, turnaround time, adherence to SOPs, documentation quality (CLIA, CAP, ISO 17025 or FDA audits), safety practice and the ability to troubleshoot an instrument. Mention validations, method comparisons, inspections you participated in and any training of new staff.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • MLT / MLS (ASCP) or relevant degree
  • Clinical chemistry / hematology / microbiology (departments)
  • Specimen processing and accessioning
  • Quality control and calibration
  • Instrumentation (Sysmex, Roche cobas, Abbott)
  • LIS / LIMS (Epic Beaker, Sunquest, LabWare)
  • PCR / ELISA / cell culture (research labs)
  • CLIA / CAP / GLP / GMP compliance
  • SOP adherence and documentation
  • Instrument troubleshooting and maintenance

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Ran the hematology and coagulation bench on nights at a 250-bed hospital, processing about 300 specimens per shift on Sysmex XN analyzers and documenting QC in Epic Beaker.
  • Example: Performed qPCR and ELISA assays for a biotech research group, maintaining GLP documentation that passed an internal audit with no findings.
  • Example: Completed a method validation for a new chemistry analyzer, including precision, linearity and correlation studies reviewed by the medical director.

Common mistakes on lab technician resumes

  • Not saying which kind of lab you worked in, or omitting certifications and state licenses that postings require.
  • Listing techniques without instruments, volumes or quality systems.
  • Leaving out LIS or LIMS experience.
  • Vague bullets like "performed lab tests" that give the supervisor nothing to assess.
