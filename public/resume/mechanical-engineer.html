How to tailor a mechanical engineer resume to the job posting

Mechanical engineering resumes are screened for the right tools, the right industry and evidence that your designs made it off the screen and into production or testing.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters commonly look for CAD tools (SolidWorks, Creo, CATIA, Siemens NX, AutoCAD), analysis tools (ANSYS, Abaqus, MATLAB), and practices such as GD&T, DFM/DFA, FEA, tolerance analysis and root cause analysis. Mention a PE or EIT/FE status if you have it and the posting values it.

Hiring managers look for the products or systems you designed, your role from concept through testing and manufacturing, and outcomes: weight or cost reduced, failures resolved, test standards met, or production ramp supported. Industry context, such as automotive, HVAC, medical devices or aerospace, often matters because of regulations and standards.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • SolidWorks / Creo / CATIA
  • GD&T
  • FEA (ANSYS, Abaqus)
  • DFM / DFA
  • Prototyping and testing
  • Root cause analysis
  • Tolerance analysis
  • Engineering drawings and BOMs
  • Manufacturing processes
  • MATLAB

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Designed an injection-molded enclosure in SolidWorks for a handheld medical device, working with the supplier on DFM changes before tooling.
  • Example: Ran FEA on a mounting bracket and redesigned it to reduce weight while meeting the required safety factor.
  • Example: Led root cause analysis on recurring pump seal failures, tracing them to a tolerance stack-up issue and updating the drawing.

Common mistakes on mechanical engineer resumes

  • Listing CAD software without describing what you designed.
  • Focusing on student projects when you have relevant professional work.
  • Leaving out the industry and standards you worked to.
