How to tailor an account manager resume to the job posting

Account managers are judged on keeping customers and growing them. Recruiters want to see the size of your book, how well you retained it, and how much additional revenue you created from existing customers.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters frequently check for account management, client retention, renewals, upselling and cross-selling, CRM tools (Salesforce, HubSpot), and terms like QBRs, account planning and customer success. Note whether the role is sales-led (with an expansion quota) or service-led, because the resumes that succeed for each are different.

Hiring managers want the shape of your portfolio: number of accounts, total revenue managed, customer size, and industry. They look for retention or renewal rates, expansion revenue, and relationships at senior levels in client organizations. If you rescued at-risk accounts, say how.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Account management
  • Client retention / renewals
  • Upselling and cross-selling
  • Salesforce / HubSpot
  • Quarterly business reviews (QBRs)
  • Account planning
  • Relationship building
  • Contract negotiation
  • Customer success
  • Revenue growth

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Managed a book of 45 mid-market accounts worth about $3M in annual recurring revenue, renewing nearly all of them during the year.
  • Example: Ran quarterly business reviews with client leadership and identified expansion opportunities that grew account revenue.
  • Example: Turned around two at-risk accounts by setting up a joint success plan and fortnightly check-ins with their operations leads.

Common mistakes on account manager resumes

  • No numbers on book size or retention.
  • Blurring account management and new business sales without saying which you did.
  • Using "built strong relationships" without evidence of what came of them.
