How to tailor a barista resume to the job posting

Coffee shops hire baristas who can keep a line moving, make consistent drinks and treat regulars well. The resume should show volume, the equipment you have used and the responsibilities you grew into, whether that was opening the store or dialing in the grinders.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Describe where you worked and how busy it was: specialty coffee shop, chain, drive-thru, bakery-cafe, hotel or campus location, and the approximate drinks or transactions per hour at peak. Name the equipment: espresso machine models (La Marzocco, Slayer, Mastrena), grinders, batch brewers, pour-over, cold brew and nitro systems, and whether you dialed in espresso and adjusted recipes for roast changes.

List skills precisely: milk steaming and latte art, drink consistency, order accuracy, cash handling, POS (Square, Toast, Clover, proprietary chain systems), food handler certification, opening and closing procedures, inventory and restocking, and keeping health department standards. If you completed any Specialty Coffee Association courses or a roaster's training program, include them.

Managers also want reliability (schedule flexibility, early openings) and customer skills: remembering regulars, handling complaints, upselling pastries or retail beans, and training new hires. If you were a shift lead, mention cash reconciliation, scheduling and ordering.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Espresso preparation and dialing in
  • Milk steaming and latte art
  • High-volume service
  • Order accuracy and speed
  • POS (Square, Toast, Clover)
  • Cash handling
  • Food handler certification
  • Opening and closing procedures
  • Inventory and restocking
  • Customer service and upselling

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Served 120+ drinks per hour at peak on a two-group La Marzocco in a downtown specialty cafe, dialing in two espresso grinders each morning.
  • Example: Opened the store four days a week, completing cash reconciliation, batch brews and bakery setup before the 6:30 a.m. rush.
  • Example: Trained seven new baristas on drink standards and the POS, and took over weekly milk and syrup ordering as shift lead.

Common mistakes on barista resumes

  • Writing "made coffee" without naming the equipment, volume or whether you dialed in espresso.
  • Omitting cash handling, POS and opening or closing responsibility.
  • Leaving out schedule availability when the posting asks for early mornings or weekends.
  • A long resume for an entry-level role; one clean page is enough.
