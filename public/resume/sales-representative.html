How to tailor a sales representative resume to the job posting

Sales is one of the few fields where hiring managers expect numbers on every resume. They want to know what you sold, to whom, how the sales cycle worked, and how you performed against quota.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Recruiters screen for the sales motion: inbound or outbound, B2B or B2C, SMB or enterprise, transactional or consultative, and the deal size. ATS filters frequently match on CRM names (Salesforce, HubSpot), methodologies (SPIN, MEDDIC, Challenger, Sandler) and terms like prospecting, pipeline and closing.

Hiring managers look for quota attainment over several periods, ranking among peers, and how you built pipeline. If your numbers were strong, state them plainly with the time period. If you lack quota history, show activity and conversion metrics instead, such as meetings booked or demos converted.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Quota attainment
  • Prospecting / cold outreach
  • Pipeline management
  • Salesforce / HubSpot CRM
  • Consultative selling
  • Negotiation and closing
  • Account management
  • Product demos
  • Territory planning
  • Sales methodology (MEDDIC, SPIN, etc.)

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Achieved 112% of annual quota in 2024 selling payroll software to businesses with 20–200 employees, ranking 3rd of 14 reps.
  • Example: Built an outbound sequence for dental practices that booked an average of 12 qualified meetings per month.
  • Example: Shortened the average sales cycle by introducing a standard discovery checklist that helped disqualify poor-fit deals earlier.

Common mistakes on sales representative resumes

  • No quota or attainment figures at all.
  • Omitting deal size and customer type, so readers can't tell if your experience transfers.
  • Claiming "top performer" without saying how that was measured.
