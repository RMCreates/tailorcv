How to tailor a UX designer resume to the job posting

UX hiring managers want to understand how you think, not only what your screens looked like. Your resume should show the problems you worked on, the research and iteration behind your designs, and what happened after launch.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters for UX roles commonly include Figma, prototyping, user research, usability testing, wireframing, information architecture, interaction design and design systems. Read the posting to see whether it leans toward research, interaction design, visual UI or product design, and weight your bullets accordingly.

Hiring managers look for the products and platforms you designed for (web, iOS, Android, enterprise tools), how you worked with product managers and engineers, and outcomes: improved task completion, fewer support tickets, higher conversion. Link a portfolio with two or three detailed case studies rather than many thumbnails. Accessibility (WCAG) experience is increasingly asked for.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Figma
  • User research
  • Usability testing
  • Wireframing and prototyping
  • Interaction design
  • Information architecture
  • Design systems
  • Accessibility (WCAG)
  • Journey mapping
  • Cross-functional collaboration

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Redesigned the claims submission flow for a mobile insurance app after usability testing with 10 policyholders, reducing abandoned claims.
  • Example: Built and documented a component library in Figma adopted by four product teams, shortening handoff to engineering.
  • Example: Ran a card-sorting study to restructure a B2B admin navigation, and reduced related support tickets after launch.

Common mistakes on UX designer resumes

  • Describing deliverables ("made wireframes") without the problem or outcome.
  • A portfolio with polished visuals but no explanation of process.
  • Claiming research experience when someone else ran all the research.
