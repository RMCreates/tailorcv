How to tailor a flight attendant resume to the job posting

Airlines receive enormous numbers of flight attendant applications and screen them against fixed requirements first, then for customer service and safety temperament. Your resume should make eligibility obvious and then show calm, service-focused experience under pressure.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

If you are already a flight attendant, lead with your FAA Certificate of Demonstrated Proficiency, aircraft types you are qualified on, your airline, base, years flying, and whether you have worked international, charter or regional operations. List safety and emergency procedures training, CPR/first aid and AED, and any lead or purser role.

If you are applying for the first time, airlines look for customer-facing experience under pressure: hospitality, restaurants, retail, healthcare, teaching or security. Describe situations where you handled difficult customers, medical or safety incidents, or worked long and irregular hours. Make sure basic requirements are visible: age and education, valid passport and ability to travel internationally, ability to relocate to a base, flexible availability including holidays and reserve schedules, and any language fluency.

Recruiters also check for a professional, concise format with no gaps unexplained, and for evidence of reliability and teamwork. Keep safety first in your wording: airlines describe the job as safety professional first, service second.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • FAA Certificate of Demonstrated Proficiency
  • Safety and emergency procedures
  • CPR / first aid / AED
  • Customer service under pressure
  • Conflict de-escalation
  • Inflight service
  • Flexible availability / reserve schedules
  • Valid passport and travel flexibility
  • Language fluency (if applicable)
  • Teamwork and reliability

  • Example: Served 200+ guests per shift in a busy hotel restaurant, handling complaints and allergy requests, and received consistent positive guest feedback.

Common mistakes on flight attendant resumes

  • Hiding whether you meet basic requirements such as passport, relocation and schedule flexibility.
  • Leading with service and appearance instead of safety and composure.
  • Listing jobs without describing how you handled pressure, conflict or emergencies.
  • Unexplained gaps or inconsistent dates, which airlines verify in background checks.
