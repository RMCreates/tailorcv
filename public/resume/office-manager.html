How to tailor a office manager resume to the job posting

Office manager is a broad title, so the resume has to define the job you actually did: how many people you supported, which functions you owned (facilities, vendors, finance admin, HR admin, events) and what you improved. Specifics are what separate you from the dozens of similar applications.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Start with scope: company size and type, number of employees and locations supported, and whether you reported to the CEO, COO, HR or finance. Then list the functions you own. Common ones include facilities and office moves, vendor and lease management, purchasing and supplies, budgets and expense reports, invoice processing with accounting, onboarding and offboarding, benefits and payroll administration support, scheduling and travel, event planning, reception oversight and safety or compliance.

Name your tools: Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, Slack, Zoom, Asana or Trello, QuickBooks or Expensify for expenses, HRIS platforms (Gusto, BambooHR, Rippling), visitor and badge systems and any procurement software. Mention budget size and the number of vendors you manage.

Hiring managers look for improvements: renegotiated contracts, lower supply costs, smoother onboarding, documented procedures, a successful office relocation or hybrid-work setup. They also want evidence that you can handle confidential information and run things without supervision.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Facilities and vendor management
  • Budget and expense management
  • Onboarding and offboarding
  • Purchasing and inventory
  • Calendar, travel and event coordination
  • Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace
  • HRIS and payroll support (Gusto, BambooHR, Rippling)
  • Accounts payable support
  • Process documentation
  • Office relocation / space planning

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Managed daily operations for a 90-person office across two floors, including facilities, 20+ vendor relationships and an annual operating budget of about $400K.
  • Example: Led an office relocation of 70 staff over one weekend, coordinating movers, IT, furniture and the landlord with no lost workdays.
  • Example: Renegotiated cleaning, coffee and supply contracts and switched expense reporting to Expensify, cutting office spend by roughly 15% year over year.

Common mistakes on office manager resumes

  • Not stating headcount, budget or vendor counts, so the scope of your role is unclear.
  • Listing every small task equally instead of grouping by the functions you own.
  • Leaving out software and HR or finance systems you work in.
  • No improvements or outcomes, only maintenance duties.
