How to tailor a bank teller resume to the job posting

Banks hire tellers who are accurate with cash, careful with compliance and comfortable talking to customers about products. The resume should show all three, with cash-handling and customer-service history front and center even if it comes from retail.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Recruiters look for prior cash handling, customer service and sales exposure. If you have been a teller before, state the transaction volume, cash limits you handled, your balancing record and the branch's size and type (retail branch, in-store branch, credit union). If you are coming from retail or food service, highlight register responsibility, balancing a drawer, handling returns and resolving customer problems.

Describe the compliance side explicitly: Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money-laundering (BSA/AML) training, Know Your Customer procedures, currency transaction reports, suspicious activity awareness, counterfeit detection, identity verification and Regulation CC hold procedures. Banks need to see that you take these seriously.

Then show referrals and relationship building: identifying customer needs, referring to bankers or loan officers, opening accounts, cross-selling cards and digital banking, and meeting referral goals. Mention the core banking or teller platform if you know it (Fiserv, Jack Henry, FIS), cash recyclers, and any licensing such as notary. Bilingual ability is a real asset in many branches.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Cash handling and drawer balancing
  • Transaction processing (deposits, withdrawals, payments)
  • BSA / AML and KYC compliance
  • Customer service
  • Product referrals and cross-selling
  • Fraud and counterfeit detection
  • Teller platform (Fiserv, Jack Henry, FIS)
  • Cash recycler / vault operations
  • Notary public
  • Bilingual (if applicable)

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Processed roughly 150 transactions per day at a high-traffic retail branch with a drawer balanced to zero on all but two days in a year.
  • Example: Completed BSA/AML and KYC training, filed currency transaction reports accurately and flagged a counterfeit bill series that led to a branch alert.
  • Example: Averaged 25 qualified referrals per month to personal bankers, consistently exceeding the branch referral goal.

Common mistakes on bank teller resumes

  • Leaving out your balancing record and transaction volume, which hiring managers use to judge accuracy.
  • Treating compliance as an afterthought instead of naming the procedures you follow.
  • Not translating retail or restaurant experience into cash handling and customer service terms.
  • Ignoring referral and sales results when the posting mentions goals.
