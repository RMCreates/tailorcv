How to tailor an administrative assistant resume to the job posting

Administrative assistant roles are about keeping an office or team running smoothly. Employers want someone organized, discreet and reliable with the specific software their office uses, and your resume should prove that with concrete details.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

ATS filters often check for Microsoft Office (especially Outlook, Word and Excel), Google Workspace, calendar management, data entry, filing systems, and sometimes typing speed. Industry-specific systems matter too: medical offices may want EHR or scheduling software, law firms may want document management or billing tools, and many offices use QuickBooks.

Hiring managers look for the size of the team you supported, the volume of work you handled (calls, appointments, invoices, meetings), and anything you improved, such as a filing system you reorganized or a process you made faster. Professional communication and confidentiality are worth showing through examples rather than adjectives.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • Microsoft Office / Google Workspace
  • Calendar and meeting scheduling
  • Data entry
  • Office management
  • Front desk / reception
  • Filing and records management
  • Travel arrangements
  • Invoice processing / QuickBooks
  • Confidential information handling
  • Written communication

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Supported a 15-person engineering office, managing calendars, ordering supplies and coordinating visitor access.
  • Example: Moved paper client files into a shared Google Drive structure with consistent naming, making documents easier to find for the whole team.
  • Example: Processed around 120 vendor invoices per month in QuickBooks and followed up on discrepancies with suppliers.

Common mistakes on administrative assistant resumes

  • Long lists of soft skills ("organized, detail-oriented, team player") with no examples.
  • Leaving out the software you know, which is often the main screening criterion.
  • Typos. For a role built on attention to detail, they are especially costly.
