How to tailor a bookkeeper resume to the job posting

Bookkeeping employers want to know which software you run, which parts of the accounting cycle you own and how clean your books are. A good bookkeeper resume reads like a list of processes you can take over on day one.

What recruiters and ATS filters look for

Name the software first: QuickBooks Online or Desktop (and whether you are a ProAdvisor), Xero, Sage, NetSuite, FreshBooks, plus payroll systems (Gusto, ADP, Paychex), bill-pay and expense tools (Bill.com, Expensify, Ramp) and Excel or Google Sheets at a specific skill level (pivot tables, lookups). Certifications such as AIPB's Certified Bookkeeper or NACPB's Certified Public Bookkeeper help, as does any accounting coursework.

Then describe scope. For each job, list the functions you handled: accounts payable and receivable, bank and credit card reconciliations, payroll processing and payroll tax filings, sales tax, 1099 preparation, journal entries, fixed assets, month-end close and financial statements. Give the size of the business (revenue, transaction volume, number of entities or clients if you worked at a bookkeeping firm) and the industry, since construction, restaurants, nonprofits and e-commerce each have their own quirks.

Employers also want to hear about accuracy and timeliness: closing the month within a set number of days, catching errors or duplicate payments, cleaning up historical books, and working with an outside CPA at year-end.

Keywords and skills to include (when they're true for you)

Only use the ones you can back up with real experience, and prefer the exact wording of the posting you're applying to.

  • QuickBooks Online / Desktop
  • Xero / Sage / NetSuite
  • Accounts payable and receivable
  • Bank reconciliations
  • Payroll processing (Gusto, ADP)
  • Month-end close
  • General ledger and journal entries
  • Sales tax and 1099 filings
  • Excel (pivot tables, lookups)
  • Financial statements

Example bullet points

These are illustrative examples, not real candidates. Use them for structure (action, context, result) and replace the details and numbers with your own.

  • Example: Maintained full-charge books for a $6M construction company in QuickBooks Desktop, including job costing, AP/AR, biweekly payroll for 35 employees and monthly close within 7 business days.
  • Example: Managed bookkeeping for 14 small-business clients at an accounting firm, reconciling bank and credit card accounts monthly and preparing year-end packages for the CPA.
  • Example: Cleaned up 18 months of uncategorized transactions and unreconciled accounts for a new client, delivering accurate financials ahead of their loan application.

Common mistakes on bookkeeper resumes

  • Not naming the software and version, which is usually the first filter.
  • Listing "bookkeeping" as a single bullet instead of the specific functions you owned.
  • Leaving out business size and industry, so employers cannot judge the complexity you have handled.
  • Overstating accounting work (audits, tax returns) you did not actually perform.
