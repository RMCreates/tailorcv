How to write a sales cover letter

Sales managers hire on numbers and process. A sales cover letter should read like a short, well-organized pitch: quota attainment, deal size, sales cycle and the methods you use, followed by a specific reason you want to sell this product to this market.

What the reader is looking for

Lead with the numbers a sales leader cares about: quota and attainment by year, revenue closed, average deal size, sales cycle length, pipeline generated, win rate, rankings on the team. Say what you sold, to whom (SMB, mid-market, enterprise, consumers), through which motion (inbound, outbound, field, channel, full-cycle or SDR) and in what cycle length. If you are early in your career, use activity metrics, conversion rates and any sales-adjacent results from retail, hospitality or fundraising.

Then show process and tools: prospecting methods, discovery frameworks (MEDDIC, SPIN, Challenger), CRM discipline (Salesforce, HubSpot), forecasting accuracy, and how you work with marketing or customer success. Finish with why this product and market, in terms that show you understand the buyer. A sales letter that cannot explain why the reader's product is worth selling has already lost the first discovery call.

A structure that works

  1. Opening (2 sentences): the role and your sales background in one line with the headline number.
  2. Results (1 paragraph): quota attainment, revenue, deal size, cycle length, rankings, with the segment and motion.
  3. Process and tools (3–4 sentences): how you prospect, qualify and forecast, and the CRM you use.
  4. Why this product and market, and close (2–3 sentences).

Example cover letter

This example is fictional: the person, employer and figures are invented to show the structure. Do not copy it; replace every detail with your own.

Dear Ms. Beaumont,

I'm applying for the Account Executive position on the mid-market team at Tidewater Scheduling. I'm a full-cycle AE who has finished at 112% and 128% of quota in the last two years selling workforce software to companies with 200 to 2,000 employees.

Last year I closed $1.4M in new annual recurring revenue on a $1.1M quota, with an average deal size of about $38K and a 71-day sales cycle. Roughly 40% of my pipeline came from my own outbound prospecting, the rest from inbound and partner referrals. I finished second of eleven on the team.

I run discovery with MEDDIC, keep Salesforce current enough that my manager uses my pipeline as the forecast example, and work closely with our solutions consultant on technical evaluations. I have sold into operations and HR leaders at hospitality and healthcare companies, which is the segment Tidewater is expanding into.

I'd welcome the chance to walk you through a recent deal and hear more about the territory. Thank you for your time.

Marcus Ellison

Common mistakes

  • No quota attainment or revenue figures, which is the first thing a sales manager looks for.
  • Describing yourself as a "hunter" or "closer" without the process behind it.
  • Not naming the segment, motion or product category you sold.
  • Failing to say anything specific about the company's product or buyers.
