How to write a cover letter with an employment gap

Gaps for caregiving, health, travel, education or a difficult job market are normal. The cover letter should address a gap briefly and factually, then show that your skills are current and that you are ready to work. Reviewers care far more about what you can do now than about the calendar.

What the reader is looking for

Address the gap in one or two sentences, in neutral language, without medical or family detail: "I took two years away from full-time work to care for a parent and returned to the workforce this spring." You do not owe a full explanation. What you do owe the reader is reassurance that your skills are current, so follow the gap sentence immediately with what you have done to keep up: courses, certifications, freelance or volunteer work, part-time roles or relevant projects.

Then write the rest of the letter as you would any other: match two or three of the posting's requirements with specific results from your prior roles, and give a concrete reason you want this job. Put your pre-gap experience in the present tense of capability ("I manage", "I build") where it is still true.

A structure that works

  1. Opening (2 sentences): the role, and a one-line summary of your relevant background.
  2. The gap (1–2 sentences): a factual, brief explanation, followed by what you have done to stay current.
  3. Evidence (1 paragraph): two or three posting requirements matched to specific results from your experience.
  4. Close (2 sentences): why this company, and a request for a conversation.

Example cover letter

This example is fictional: the person, employer and figures are invented to show the structure. Do not copy it; replace every detail with your own.

Dear Ms. Nakamura,

I'm applying for the Staff Accountant position at Greyfield Dental Partners. I have seven years of accounting experience in multi-location healthcare groups, most recently as a senior accountant for a physical therapy chain.

I stepped away from full-time work for 18 months to care for a family member and returned to the workforce this year. During that time I kept my CPA license active, completed 40 hours of continuing education with a focus on revenue recognition, and handled monthly close on a contract basis for a two-location veterinary practice.

Your posting asks for month-end close across multiple entities, intercompany reconciliations and QuickBooks Online. In my last full-time role I closed the books for 11 clinic entities within six business days each month, built the intercompany reconciliation schedule the team still uses, and led the migration from QuickBooks Desktop to Online.

Greyfield's growth from 6 to 20 practices is the type of environment where I do my best work. I would welcome the chance to discuss the role. Thank you.

Samuel Adeyemi

Common mistakes

  • Over-explaining the gap with personal or medical details the employer does not need.
  • Leaving the gap unaddressed when it is recent and obvious, which makes reviewers guess.
  • Describing the gap defensively or apologizing for it.
  • Forgetting to show that your skills are current with specific activities.
