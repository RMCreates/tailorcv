How to write a cover letter for an internal promotion

An internal cover letter is not a formality. The hiring manager may know your name but not your results, and the panel may include people who have never worked with you. Write it as a case for the new role, not a summary of your tenure.

What the reader is looking for

The mistake most internal candidates make is assuming their work speaks for itself. It does not, especially when HR runs a standard process and external candidates are in the mix. Treat the letter as a chance to put your results in writing: what you have delivered in your current role, what you already do that overlaps with the new job, and what you have learned about the company that an outside hire would take months to pick up.

Be specific about the new role's requirements and address them one by one. If you have already acted in the role, covered for the previous holder or led a project at that level, say so. If there is a gap, acknowledge it briefly and describe how you would close it.

A structure that works

  1. Opening (2 sentences): the role you are applying for, your current title and team, and how long you have been with the company.
  2. Results in your current role (1 paragraph): two or three concrete outcomes with numbers, chosen because they relate to the new job.
  3. Fit for the new role (1 paragraph): the posting's main requirements, how you already meet them, and the institutional knowledge you bring.
  4. Close (2 sentences): why you want this specific step, and a request to discuss it.

Example cover letter

This example is fictional: the person, employer and figures are invented to show the structure. Do not copy it; replace every detail with your own.

Dear Mr. Lindqvist,

I'm applying for the Customer Success Team Lead position posted this week. I have been a Senior Customer Success Manager on the mid-market team at Fernhollow Software for three and a half years.

In that time my book of 45 accounts has renewed at 94% or higher each year, and I led the migration of our onboarding checklists into the new CRM, which cut the average time-to-first-value for new customers from 41 days to 29. For the last five months I have also covered the weekly team pipeline review while the previous lead was on leave.

The posting emphasizes coaching, forecasting and working with Product on escalations. I have mentored two new CSMs through their first renewals, I already own the renewal forecast for the mid-market segment, and I run our monthly escalation review with the product managers. I know our customers, our systems and the gaps in our playbooks, and I would like to use that to help the team hit next year's net retention target.

I would welcome the chance to discuss the role with you and the panel. Thank you.

Priya Raman

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the panel knows your work, and writing a short note instead of a real letter.
  • Framing the application as being owed the promotion because of tenure.
  • Criticizing the current team, manager or the previous holder of the role.
  • Ignoring the posting's requirements because you think the process is a formality.
