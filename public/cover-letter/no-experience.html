How to write a cover letter with no experience

When you have no job history, the cover letter matters more than the resume, because it is the only place you can explain what you would bring. The trick is to use what you have, such as coursework, projects, volunteering, sports or part-time work, as evidence rather than as excuses.

What the reader is looking for

Employers hiring for entry-level roles are not expecting a track record. They are looking for signs that you will show up, learn quickly, follow instructions and be pleasant to work with. Your letter should give concrete proof of those things. A class project you finished on time, a volunteer role where you handled money, a part-time shift where you were trusted to close, a team you captained: each of those is evidence if you describe what you actually did.

Read the posting and pick the two or three things it asks for that you can genuinely speak to. Then match each one to a specific example. Keep it factual and avoid inflated language. If the role requires something you have not done, do not pretend; mention a related thing you have done and that you would learn the rest quickly.

A structure that works

  1. Opening (2 sentences): the role, where you saw it, and one line on why you want this job in particular.
  2. Evidence (1–2 paragraphs): two or three posting requirements, each matched to something you have done in school, volunteering, activities or part-time work.
  3. Availability and practicalities (1–2 sentences): hours you can work, start date, transportation or certifications if the posting mentions them.
  4. Close (1–2 sentences): a polite request for an interview and thanks.

Example cover letter

This example is fictional: the person, employer and figures are invented to show the structure. Do not copy it; replace every detail with your own.

Dear Ms. Alvarado,

I'm writing to apply for the part-time Front Desk Assistant position at Brightwater Community Pool. I'm a second-year student at the local community college and would like a job where I deal with people all day.

Your posting asks for someone reliable who can handle check-ins, cash and questions from members. For the past two summers I have volunteered at the county fair's ticket booth, handling cash and card payments for several hundred visitors a day and reconciling the drawer at close. I also served as treasurer of my high school's robotics club, where I tracked a budget of about $4,000 and kept receipts in order for our advisor.

I'm available evenings and weekends during the semester and full time over the summer, and I hold a current CPR certification from my lifeguard course.

I would appreciate the chance to interview and can start at short notice. Thank you for your time.

Mateo Reyes

Common mistakes

  • Starting with "Although I have no experience...", which frames everything that follows as a weakness.
  • Writing about your personality in the abstract instead of describing things you have done.
  • Leaving out availability and practical details that entry-level employers need.
  • A letter longer than one page for a role that only needs three short paragraphs.
