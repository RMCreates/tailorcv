How to write a cover letter for a remote job

Remote postings attract many more applicants than local ones, so the letter has to do two things quickly: prove you fit the role and prove you can work independently across distance. Vague enthusiasm for working from home does neither.

What the reader is looking for

Remote hiring managers worry about communication, self-direction and time zones. Address all three with evidence rather than claims. Describe previous remote or distributed work, the tools you used (Slack, Notion, Jira, Zoom, Loom, GitHub, HubSpot), how you kept stakeholders informed, and how you handled asynchronous work with colleagues in other regions. If you have not worked remotely before, point to times you delivered without close supervision, such as field roles, independent projects or managing your own client book.

Be clear about practicalities the posting asks for: your time zone, the hours you can overlap, your location and work authorization, and whether you have a reliable home office. Then spend the rest of the letter on the role itself, with two or three specific results that match the requirements. Remote employers still hire primarily for the job.

A structure that works

  1. Opening (2 sentences): the role and your relevant background in one line.
  2. Role fit (1 paragraph): two or three posting requirements matched to specific results.
  3. Remote readiness (1 short paragraph): prior remote or independent work, tools, communication habits, time zone and overlap.
  4. Close (1–2 sentences): why this company and a request to talk.

Example cover letter

This example is fictional: the person, employer and figures are invented to show the structure. Do not copy it; replace every detail with your own.

Dear Hiring Manager,

I'm applying for the remote Technical Support Engineer position at Lanternmoor Analytics. I have spent five years supporting B2B SaaS customers, the last three of them fully remote.

Your posting asks for someone who can resolve tier 2 tickets involving APIs and data integrations and write documentation that reduces repeat questions. At my current company I handle about 60 escalated tickets a week, most involving REST API and webhook issues, with a customer satisfaction score consistently above 4.7 out of 5. I wrote the integration troubleshooting guide that cut tickets on our two most common errors by about a third.

I work from Lisbon and regularly overlap with colleagues in New York and Austin from 2 p.m. to 8 p.m. my time. My team is spread across four time zones, so I'm used to documenting decisions in Notion, recording Loom walkthroughs instead of scheduling calls, and keeping ticket updates clear enough that the next person can pick them up without asking.

I would welcome a conversation about the role. Thank you for your time.

Ines Carvalho

Common mistakes

  • Leading with how much you want to work from home rather than what you would do for the company.
  • Not stating your time zone, location or overlap when the posting asks for it.
  • Claiming to be "self-motivated" without describing how you actually work remotely.
  • Treating the remote part as the whole letter and neglecting the role's requirements.
