How to write a software engineer cover letter

Many engineering teams skip cover letters, but when one is read, it is read by a hiring manager looking for signal the resume does not give: why this team, what you actually built, and whether you can explain technical work clearly. Keep it short and specific.

What the reader is looking for

Write for an engineer. Open with the role, your current stack and years of experience, then go straight to one or two things you built that relate to the posting. Describe the problem, what you did and the result in a sentence or two each, with technologies and numbers: requests per second, latency, users, reduction in incidents, time saved. This is the part that gets you the interview.

Then say why this company and team, in a way that shows you looked: a product you use, an engineering blog post, an open-source project, the problem domain or the scale. Keep it to two or three sentences and avoid flattery. If there is an obvious gap, such as a language the posting lists that you have not used professionally, address it in one sentence with related experience. Close with a link to your GitHub or portfolio and a direct request to talk.

A structure that works

  1. Opening (2 sentences): the role, your current stack and experience level.
  2. Shipped work (1–2 short paragraphs): one or two projects relevant to the posting, each with the problem, your contribution and a measurable result.
  3. Why this team (2–3 sentences): a specific, researched reason.
  4. Close (1–2 sentences): links, availability and a request to talk.

Example cover letter

This example is fictional: the person, employer and figures are invented to show the structure. Do not copy it; replace every detail with your own.

Hi Backend Engineering team at Orrin Bay Payments,

I'm applying for the Senior Backend Engineer role. I have seven years of experience, the last four building payment and ledger services in Go and PostgreSQL on AWS.

Your posting mentions reliability of settlement under load. At my current company I redesigned our settlement service from a nightly batch into an idempotent, event-driven pipeline on Kafka, which cut settlement latency from hours to under five minutes and removed a class of duplicate-payout incidents entirely; it now processes about 2 million transfers a day. I also led the migration of our ledger to a double-entry model with reconciliation checks that run in CI, which caught three real bugs before release.

I'm interested in Orrin Bay specifically because of your public write-up on exactly-once processing and because your platform targets the marketplace payouts problem my team spent two years on. I have not used Rust professionally, but I have shipped a small Rust CLI and I'm comfortable reading it.

My GitHub is linked on my resume. I would enjoy talking through the settlement architecture with the team.

Theo Marchetti

Common mistakes

  • Repeating the resume's skills list instead of describing one or two systems in depth.
  • Generic praise for the company instead of a specific, technical reason for applying.
  • No numbers or outcomes attached to the work you describe.
  • Long paragraphs; engineers skim, so keep it to four short ones.
